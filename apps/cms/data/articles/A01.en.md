::: texte
Four years of review, and four years of public discussion about the same thing: the view. From the hamlet of Écharmes, six 180-metre masts along the ridge do change a landscape. That is what the meetings were about, and what the opinion pieces were about, and in all likelihood what November's public inquiry will be about too.

Meanwhile, the question that will actually decide the project has been asked in no room at all. It fits in one line of an eighty-page technical file, lodged in 2024 and consultable ever since: the residual capacity of the substation the farm must connect to.
:::

::: chiffres-cles
19.8 | MW | installed capacity of the project
6 | turbines | along the ridge, at 1,512 m
1,512 | m | altitude of the Trois-Vents pass

:::

::: texte
## A substation does not stretch

A wind farm produces nothing if it cannot feed in. Feeding in goes through a substation — the point where the distribution grid meets the transmission grid. Every substation has a hosting capacity, it is published, and it is consumed as connections are accepted.

The one serving the plateau is forty kilometres away. Its residual capacity, at the last published state, does not cover 19.8 megawatts. It covers less than half.
:::

::: image-legendee image=blocs/A01-poste-source.svg legende="The extract from the connection file where the substation's residual capacity appears." credit="Graphic reconstruction — The Highland Echo"
:::

::: texte
Two ways out, and only one is being discussed. Either the project is sized to what the substation will take, and loses two turbines. Or the substation is reinforced, and someone pays for that reinforcement — an amount that appears in none of the motions passed so far by the villages involved.

This is not an objection to the project. It is the observation that the variable governing its size, and therefore its economics, has never been put on the table where the councillors deliberate.
:::

::: encadre titre="The review timetable, as it actually ran" variante=complement
- **2022** — environmental consent application lodged.
- **2024** — grid connection file lodged; the substation's residual capacity is written in it.
- **2025** — environmental authority opinion, two reservations, none about the connection.
- **November 2026** — public inquiry.

The order matters: the technical question arrived two years before the moment the public will be consulted.
:::

::: texte
## What the inquiry can still change

It can change the siting at the margins, the mitigation measures, the acoustic curtailment hours. It will not reopen the installed capacity, fixed in the file as lodged.

Put plainly: November's meeting will be about what is left once the decisive question has been settled elsewhere, by a document nobody asked to see. It has been public for two years.
:::
